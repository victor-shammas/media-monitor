## 1. Executive summary

Between 2021 and 2026 the Norwegian Progress Party (Fremskrittspartiet, FrP) travelled from its worst parliamentary result in nearly three decades to its best result ever. Under Sylvi Listhaug, who took over the leadership in May 2021 promising a *"skarpere Frp"* ("a sharper FrP"), the party fell to 11.6% in the September 2021 Storting election, began recovering in the 2023 local elections (11.4%, its first electoral advance since 2011), topped national polls from autumn 2024, and won 23.9% and 47 seats in the 8 September 2025 Storting election — overtaking Høyre to become Norway's second-largest party and the dominant force of the Norwegian right. By spring 2026 individual polls placed FrP near 29–30%.

Five findings structure this report:

**First**, FrP remains the most contested classificatory case in the European radical-right literature. Cas Mudde's canonical framework excludes it from the populist radical right family, treating it as a "neoliberal populist" party; Nordic scholars (Jungar & Jupskås, Bjørklund, Ivarsflaten) include it with qualifications as the *least radical* member of the Nordic populist right. The 2021–2026 period complicates both readings: the party's immigration program radicalized substantially (zero border asylum, third-country processing, a quota-refugee stop, a Danish-style deportation reform), while its leader simultaneously softened her public register and the party attracted historic levels of support from young voters and even immigrant-background voters.

**Second**, crime became FrP's signature mobilizing issue. The "svenske tilstander" ("Swedish conditions") frame — long used as a warning — was declared realized by Listhaug after a hand-grenade explosion in central Oslo in September 2025 involving 13-year-old suspects. FrP converted this agenda into concrete parliamentary victories from opposition, most notably the February 2025 Storting majority instructing the government to introduce stop-and-search "visitation zones" and doubled sentencing frames for gang crime.

**Third**, FrP fuses welfare-chauvinist "sustainability" framing with statistical politics on immigrant crime: the party commissioned and publicized SSB tables (December 2024) showing immigrant overrepresentation in criminal charges, demanded a public *"innvandringsregnskap"* (immigration cost account), and proposed expelling all foreign citizens convicted of serious crime regardless of their ties to Norway.

**Fourth**, on climate FrP is best described not as a denialist party but as an *obstruction* party with a tolerated denialist wing (most prominently honorary chairman Carl I. Hagen). Its 2025–2029 program rejects Norway's enhanced Paris targets; in office-adjacent parliamentary practice in 2025–2026 it helped repeal Norway's 2030 climate-neutrality goal, pause the flagship floating offshore-wind project, push for new oil licensing rounds including impact assessment of Lofoten–Vesterålen–Senja, and stop electrification of the continental shelf.

**Fifth**, the "Norwegian MAGA" label, applied mainly by foreign media around the 2025 election, fits FrP only partially. The party shares MAGA's anti-elite, anti-woke, pro-fossil and anti-immigration repertoire, and its youth wing and parts of its base are openly Trump-enthusiastic (Nobel Peace Prize nominations for Trump by FrP MPs; FpU leader Simen Velle saying he would have voted for Trump). But the leadership has repeatedly broken with Trump — Listhaug called him unfit for office after January 6, condemned his treatment of Zelensky in March 2025, and criticized his tariffs — and FrP's firm Atlanticism and strong support for Ukraine separate it sharply from the MAGA-aligned European right (AfD, FPÖ). Norwegian political scientists largely reject the "MAGA-fication" reading of the 2025 result, attributing FrP's surge to domestic grievances: cost of living, taxes, electricity prices, immigration, and crime.

## 2. Scope, sources, and method

This report covers FrP from the leadership transition of May 2021 through early June 2026, with historical background where needed (the party's 1973 founding, the 2013–2020 government participation, the 2018 Listhaug resignation, the January 2020 government exit). It was produced through a structured research process: five parallel thematic searches (general policies/elections; European right and MAGA; nativism/immigration; crime; climate/energy) across roughly 90 web searches in Norwegian and English, followed by an adversarial verification pass on the most load-bearing claims.

Source hierarchy: official records (Stortinget, Valgdirektoratet, regjeringen.no, SSB), the party's own program and policy documents (frp.no), Norwegian quality press (NRK, Aftenposten, VG, Dagsavisen, TV 2, Vårt Land, Klassekampen, Altinget, Nettavisen), fact-checkers (Faktisk.no), and academic literature (C-REX/University of Oslo, Institutt for samfunnsforskning, peer-reviewed journals). Two caveats: (a) many Norwegian sites blocked automated full-text retrieval, so some quotations rest on search-result extracts of those pages and are flagged in §12; (b) where sources conflicted (e.g., FrP's final 2025 seat count), the conflict is documented and resolved with the most authoritative source.

## 3. Background: party history and the scholarly classification debate

### 3.1 Origins and trajectory

FrP descends from **Anders Langes Parti til sterk nedsettelse av skatter, avgifter og offentlige inngrep** ("Anders Lange's Party for a Strong Reduction in Taxes, Duties and Public Intervention"), founded in 1973 as an anti-tax protest party. This libertarian genealogy — rather than the fascist or ethno-nationalist lineages of many continental radical-right parties — is central to the classification debate. Under Carl I. Hagen (leader 1978–2006) the party added immigration restriction to its repertoire from the late 1980s; under Siv Jensen (2006–2021) it professionalized, peaked at 22.9% in 2009, and entered government in 2013 as junior partner in Erna Solberg's coalition — the first Nordic populist-right party to govern in a *bourgeois coalition* ([Bjerkem 2016, European View](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1007/s12290-016-0404-8); [snl.no: Fremskrittspartiet](https://snl.no/Fremskrittspartiet)).

FrP governed for nearly seven years (2013–2020), holding the finance, justice, immigration, transport, and petroleum portfolios at various times, before leaving voluntarily on 20 January 2020 in protest at the government-assisted repatriation of a Norwegian woman suspected of IS affiliation and her two children from Syria. Siv Jensen: "I'm doing it because it's the only right thing to do" ([CNN, 20 Jan 2020](https://edition.cnn.com/2020/01/20/europe/norway-government-collapse-isis-intl/index.html); [Al Jazeera, 20 Jan 2020](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2020/1/20/norway-finance-minister-quits-over-isil-womans-return-from-syria)).

A biographical fact relevant to the party's relationship with the radical-right label: **Anders Behring Breivik**, perpetrator of the 22 July 2011 attacks, was an FrP/FpU member roughly 1997–2006/07, led a local FpU branch in 2002, and left the party he found "too moderate" ([The World/PRX, 2011](https://theworld.org/dispatch/news/regions/europe/110724/europe-right-wing-political-parties-Breivik-manifesto); [openDemocracy](https://www.opendemocracy.net/en/norwegian-shift-to-right-10-short-lessons-on-progress-party/)). The party has fought the "Breivik party" framing ever since; Listhaug's line *"Vi tar avstand fra ekstremisme, men vi lar oss aldri kneble"* ("We distance ourselves from extremism, but we will never be muzzled") captures its double movement of boundary-drawing and grievance ([Nettavisen debate piece](https://www.nettavisen.no/norsk-debatt/vi-tar-avstand-fra-ekstremisme-men-vi-lar-oss-aldri-kneble/o/5-95-268134)).

### 3.2 The classification debate: populist radical right or neoliberal populist?

**The exclusionist position.** Cas Mudde's framework in *Populist Radical Right Parties in Europe* (Cambridge University Press, 2007) defines the family by nativism, authoritarianism, and populism, and treats FrP as a borderline case better classified as **"neo-liberal populist"** because its founding DNA is economic-libertarian rather than nativist ([Cambridge UP](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/populist-radical-right-parties-in-europe/244D86C50E6D1DC44C86C4D1D313F16D); Mudde's C-REX working paper ["The Study of Populist Radical Right Parties"](https://www.sv.uio.no/c-rex/english/publications/working-paper/Cas%20Mudde:%20The%20Study%20of%20Populist%20Radical%20Right%20Parties.pdf)). *(Verbatim page-level wording not independently verified in this research; the characterization is consistently reproduced across academic secondary sources — see §12.)*

**The inclusionist position.** Ann-Cathrine Jungar and Anders Ravik Jupskås ("Populist Radical Right Parties in the Nordic Region: A New and Distinct Party Family?", *Scandinavian Political Studies* 2014) include FrP in the Nordic populist radical right family but find it "less authoritarian and more economically right-wing than its sister parties in Scandinavia, but equally anti-establishment and anti-immigration" ([Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/1467-9477.12024)). Johan Bjerkem (2016) calls FrP "a hybrid between a populist radical right party and a more traditional conservative party" ([European View](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1007/s12290-016-0404-8)). Tor Bjørklund and Jørgen Goul Andersen treated the Norwegian and Danish Progress Parties within the Scandinavian anti-immigration lineage — and, notably, coined the concept of **welfare chauvinism** on precisely these two parties ("Structural changes and new cleavages: The Progress parties in Denmark and Norway", 1990; cf. [Careja & Harris, *Journal of European Social Policy* 2022](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/09589287211068796)). Jens Rydgren's *Oxford Handbook of the Radical Right* (OUP 2018) covers FrP within the field ([OUP](https://global.oup.com/academic/product/the-oxford-handbook-of-the-radical-right-9780190274559)), and Elisabeth Ivarsflaten's influential work on anti-immigration party success uses FrP as a core case (e.g., her chapter in Rydgren, ed., *Class Politics and the Radical Right*, Routledge 2013).

**The Norwegian/C-REX synthesis.** In the Norwegian literature FrP is most often labelled *høyrepopulistisk* (right-wing populist), located — in a formulation associated with Jupskås and echoed by Cathrine Thorleifsson — "in the borderland between the populist radical right and the more centrist-conservative" ([Jupskås, C-REX working paper 2021](https://www.sv.uio.no/c-rex/english/publications/working-paper/2021-01-jupskas.pdf); [C-REX: "What is right-wing radicalism?"](https://www.sv.uio.no/c-rex/english/resources/key-consepts-and-questions/what-is-right-wing-radicalism.html)). A distinctive Norwegian contribution is the **"safety-valve" thesis**: C-REX-affiliated researchers argue that FrP's existence *absorbs* radical-right sentiment into mainstream parliamentary politics, which helps explain why organized extra-parliamentary radical-right movements are weaker in Norway than in Sweden ([Nettavisen: "Forskere: Frp gjør det vanskeligere å være høyreradikal i Norge"](https://www.nettavisen.no/nyheter/forskere-frp-gjor-det-vanskeligere-a-vare-hoyreradikal-i-norge/s/12-95-3424145992)). The critical anthropological counter-view — that FrP normalized far-right discourse in Norway — is represented by Sindre Bangstad ("The Rise of the Populist Right in Norway", *Boston Review*, 2017).

**Ivarsflaten's "reputational shield."** Ivarsflaten's argument ("Reputational Shields: Why Most Anti-Immigrant Parties Failed in Western Europe, 1980–2005") is essential for understanding FrP: anti-immigration parties succeed durably only when an ideological legacy shields them from racism accusations. FrP's anti-tax origin functions as exactly such a shield — a point that recurs below in the analysis of Listhaug's simultaneous programmatic radicalization and rhetorical softening ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228468169_Reputational_shields_Why_most_anti-immigrant_parties_failed_in_Western_Europe_1980-2005)).

**Moderation through inclusion.** Comparative work on radical-right parties in government (Tjitske Akkerman, "Comparing Radical Right Parties in Government", *West European Politics* 2012; Akkerman, de Lange & Rooduijn, eds., *Radical Right-Wing Populist Parties in Western Europe: Into the Mainstream?*, Routledge 2016) treats FrP's 2013–2020 governing period as a paradigm case of moderation through inclusion: the absence of a *cordon sanitaire* "may explain why the party developed a more moderate and pragmatic approach than most other right-wing populist parties" ([Bjerkem 2016](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12290-016-0404-8)). At the same time, the coalition delivered an unprecedentedly restrictive immigration policy and made Norway a European leader in forced deportations — i.e., moderation in style coexisted with restriction in output ([Bangstad, Boston Review](https://www.bostonreview.net/articles/sindre-bangstad-norway-populist-right/)).

## 4. Leadership and electoral trajectory, 2021–2026

### 4.1 Sylvi Listhaug: profile and takeover

Sylvi Listhaug (b. 25 December 1977, raised on a farm in Ørskog, Møre og Romsdal) served in the Solberg government as Minister of Agriculture (2013–2015), **Minister of Immigration and Integration (December 2015 – March 2018)**, briefly Minister of Justice (2018), Minister of the Elderly and Public Health (2019), and Minister of Petroleum and Energy (December 2019 – January 2020) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvi_Listhaug); [snl.no](https://snl.no/Sylvi_Listhaug)). When she was appointed oil minister, PM Solberg had to insist "Sylvi is not a climate denier" ([fvn.no](https://www.fvn.no/norgeogverden/i/0nJpro/endringer-i-regjeringen)).

Her defining pre-leadership episode came on 9 March 2018, when as Justice Minister she posted on Facebook: *"Ap mener terroristenes rettigheter er viktigere enn nasjonens sikkerhet. Lik og del!"* ("Labour thinks terrorists' rights are more important than the nation's security. Like and share!"), over an image of masked armed fighters — in the context of a parliamentary vote on stripping suspected foreign fighters of citizenship without court proceedings. Labour leader Jonas Gahr Støre accused her of "feeding the hatred" behind the 22 July 2011 massacre of Labour youth; facing a no-confidence vote that threatened the government, she resigned on 20 March 2018 ([NRK](https://www.nrk.no/norge/listhaug-sletter-omstridt-facebook-innlegg-1.13961460); [Aftenposten](https://www.aftenposten.no/kultur/i/G1E0qQ/11-dager-etter-at-hun-postet-dette-bildet-maatte-sylvi-listhaug-gaa-av); [Euronews, 20 Mar 2018](https://www.euronews.com/2018/03/20/norway-s-justice-minister-quits-her-position-over-her-facebook-comments)). International coverage at the time routinely dubbed her "the Norwegian Trump"; as immigration minister she had denounced the *"godhetstyranniet"* ("tyranny of kindness") of the humanitarian establishment ([Jacobin, "The Fall of the Norwegian Trump", April 2018](https://jacobin.com/2018/04/norway-far-right-sylvi-listhaug-immigration)).

**Listhaug was elected party leader at the national convention on 8 May 2021** (parliamentary leader 12 May 2021), with Ketil Solvik-Olsen and Terje Søviknes as deputies, after Siv Jensen designated her as successor ([Vårt Land, 12 May 2021](https://www.vl.no/nyheter/2021/05/12/listhaug-valgt-til-parlamentarisk-leder-i-frp/); [snl.no](https://snl.no/Fremskrittspartiet)). She promised a *"skarpere Frp"* ([Klar Tale, 7 May 2021](https://www.klartale.no/norge/2021/05/07/listhaug-vil-ha-et-skarpere-frp/)) and used her first leader's speech to attack *"klimahysteri"* (climate hysteria) and the Green Party ([VG, May 2021](https://www.vg.no/nyheter/innenriks/i/we1JM4/listhaug-til-frps-landsmoete-ut-mot-klimahysteri-og-mdg)).

### 4.2 2021: the trough

In the 13 September 2021 Storting election — four months into Listhaug's leadership — FrP won **11.6% and 21 seats (−6)**, its worst result since 1993 ([Wikipedia: 2021 Norwegian parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Norwegian_parliamentary_election); [DN, Sept 2021](https://www.dn.no/kommentar/valg-2021/frp/listhaug-leder-frp-til-det-darligste-valget-siden-1993/2-1-1066789)). Labour's Jonas Gahr Støre formed a minority coalition with the Centre Party (Sp); FrP entered full opposition.

### 4.3 2023–2024: turnaround

The 2023 municipal elections gave FrP 11.4% (+3.2 pp) — its first electoral advance in twelve years ([VG valgnatt 2023](https://www.vg.no/valgnatt/2023/ko/partier/FRP); [Altinget](https://www.altinget.no/helse/artikkel/fremskrittspartiet-og-listhaug-gikk-fra-motvind-til-medvind)). From roughly September 2024 FrP repeatedly topped national polls: 21.9% in September 2024, 23.3% in October, and a then-record 24.2% (InFact/Nettavisen) in November 2024, well ahead of Høyre ([Dagsavisen, 7 Sept 2024](https://www.dagsavisen.no/nyheter/innenriks/2024/09/07/frp-fosser-fram-beste-maling-pa-seks-ar/); [Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/nyheter/frp-leder-sylvi-listhaug-leder-na-landets-klart-storste-parti/s/5-95-2109902); [pollofpolls.no](https://www.pollofpolls.no/?cmd=Partier&parti=Frp)). Labour rebounded in spring 2025 after Jens Stoltenberg's return as finance minister, and some pre-election polls showed FrP setbacks ([Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/nyheter/ny-maling-kraftig-tilbakegang-for-frp/s/5-95-2586147)).

**Why the surge?** Analysts converge on four drivers: (a) economic grievance — cost of living, taxes, and electricity prices channelled into populist momentum (ECPS interview with election researcher [Lise Bjånesøy, University of Bergen](https://www.populismstudies.org/dr-bjanesoy-frp-turns-economic-frustration-in-norway-into-populist-momentum/)); (b) the collapse of Høyre (−5.7 pp), with right-bloc voters migrating to FrP, aggravated by the unresolved prime-minister question (Høyre's own voters preferred Støre to Listhaug as PM; Solberg first rejected, then opened to serving under Listhaug) ([NRK](https://www.nrk.no/norge/hoyre-topper-tror-mer-pa-store-enn-listhaug-som-statsminister-1.17516987); [Dagbladet](https://www.dagbladet.no/nyheter/solberg-apner-for-listhaug/82460251)); (c) exceptional mobilization of young voters, especially young men, partly via TikTok (below); and (d) Listhaug's deliberately "de-demonized," more mainstream image — commentators noted that Støre's "fear campaign" against her had lost potency ([Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/norsk-debatt/frp-fosser-frem-og-listhaug-er-ikke-lenger-skummel/s/5-95-2491121)).

### 4.5 The voter base: gender gap, youth surge, and an immigrant-voter shift

- **SSB's Velgerundersøkelsen 2025**: just over 1 in 4 voters overall thought about immigration when voting; among FrP voters, about 70% did — the most immigration-driven electorate of any party ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/valg/artikler-og-publikasjoner/droyt-1-av-4-tenkte-pa-innvandring-da-de-stemte); [forskning.no](https://www.forskning.no/politikk/frp-er-valgvinner-hva-skjedde/2549817)).
- **Gender/age**: 38% of men under 35 voted FrP in 2025 versus 17% of women under 35 (up from 15% and 7% in 2021) — among the largest gender gaps ever recorded in Norwegian elections. FrP was the largest party among first-time voters, won the national school election with 26%, and was uniquely successful at mobilizing previous non-voters ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/valg/stortingsvalg/statistikk/stortingsvalget-velgerundersokelsen/artikler/4-av-10-byttet-parti); [Aftenposten](https://www.aftenposten.no/norge/politikk/i/2p17jy/fire-av-ti-byttet-parti-ved-stortingsvalget-frp-fikk-folk-ut-av-sofaen); [NRK](https://www.nrk.no/mr/stortingsvalget-2025_-stor-splid-mellom-unge-menn-og-kvinner---flere-unge-menn-heller-mot-frp-1.17542106); [The Local Norway, 10 Sept 2025](https://www.thelocal.no/20250910/why-is-norways-anti-immigration-progress-party-so-popular-with-young-men)).
- **Media pathway**: the probability of voting FrP was 28% among young men whose main news source is social media versus 13% among young women; researchers at the Institutt for samfunnsforskning link the youth surge to algorithmic video content and FpU leader Simen Velle's TikTok outreach ([ISF: "Hvem stemmer på Frp?"](https://www.samfunnsforskning.no/aktuelt/nyheter/2025/hvem-stemmer-pa-frp.html); [Finansavisen, 5 Jul 2024](https://www.finansavisen.no/politikk/2024/07/05/8149953/simen-velle-23aringen-bak-ny-bla-bolge)).
- **Immigrant voters**: Aftenposten reported that FrP became the *second-largest party among immigrant voters* in 2025 — a historic shift that complicates a pure-nativism reading of the party's appeal; Listhaug actively courted "well-integrated" immigrants in the campaign ([Aftenposten](https://www.aftenposten.no/norge/politikk/i/kwVkPj/fremskrittspartiet-er-nest-stoerst-blant-innvandrere); [Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/norsk-debatt/sylvi-listhaugs-valgkamp-twist-innvandrere-kan-sikre-frp-seier/o/5-95-2252271)).
- **Classic profile**: FrP continues to over-represent men, lower education levels, private-sector and manual occupations, and southern/western coastal districts; it is weakest among highly educated urban women — consistent with Ivarsflaten's findings on internalized anti-prejudice norms and women's avoidance of the radical right ([ISF Valgforskning](https://www.samfunnsforskning.no/valg/partiene/frp.html); [Ivarsflaten et al., *British Journal of Political Science*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/british-journal-of-political-science/article/abs/why-women-avoid-the-radical-right-internalized-norms-and-party-reputations/21999440F5879E6B9A20FED6C177F300)). Only 9.3% of academics voted FrP in 2025, versus over 22% of all voters ([Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/norsk-debatt/eliten-tar-feil-folket-har-karet-sylvi-listhaug-til-valgkampens-vinner/s/5-95-2563593)).

### 4.6 Key figures besides Listhaug

- **Hans Andreas Limi** — veteran finance spokesman; 1st deputy leader since the April 2023 convention (succeeding Solvik-Olsen), re-elected 2025; co-launched the 2025 crime package ([NRK](https://www.nrk.no/nyheter/hans-andreas-limi-er-valgt-som-ny-1.-nestleder-i-frp-1.16393121)).
- **Terje Søviknes** — 2nd deputy leader since 2021.
- **Per-Willy Amundsen** — MP (Troms), ex-Justice Minister (2017–18), chair of the Storting justice committee 2021–2024 and FrP's most aggressive law-and-order voice; resigned the chairmanship 10 March 2024 after an Islamophobic and misogynistic Facebook episode (§7.5).
- **Erlend Wiborg** — immigration-policy spokesperson; architect of the citizenship-tightening and deportation-reform proposals (§6.1).
- **Simen Velle** — FpU leader 2022–2026, elected MP for Oslo 2025; ignited a national gender debate in February 2024 with statements that dating culture had become "tinderfied" and the women's movement had gone too far at young men's expense ("When a young man raises his problems, he is laughed at"); said he would have voted for Trump; widely credited with FrP's TikTok-driven youth surge ([VG](https://www.vg.no/nyheter/i/GM4obQ/simen-velle-i-debatten-det-er-paa-tide-at-guttas-perspektiv-loeftes); [Morgenbladet, 1 Mar 2024](https://www.morgenbladet.no/aktuelt/2024/03/01/simen-velle-nar-en-ung-mann-lofter-sine-problemer-blir-det-ledd-av/); [snl.no](https://snl.no/Simen_Velle)).

- **Carl I. Hagen** — honorary chairman, MP 2021–2025 (Oppland levelling seat), Norway's most prominent political climate denier (§8.2).

## 5. Programmatic profile and populist rhetoric, 2021–2026

### 5.1 The programs

The 2025–2029 program, adopted at the May 2025 convention, is titled **"– mer frihet"** ("more freedom") ([program PDF](https://www.frp.no/files/Program/2025/FrP-Partiprogram-2025-2029.pdf)). Core economic planks, as expressed in the program and FrP's alternative 2025 state budget:

- Large cuts in taxes and fees as the overarching goal; a smaller public sector; more privatization and competitive tendering ([snl.no](https://snl.no/Fremskrittspartiet)).
- Raising the tax-free personal allowance toward NOK 150,000; **abolition of the wealth tax** (formuesskatt) as a stated goal — Listhaug argues it "discriminates against Norwegian ownership" — with the threshold raised from NOK 1.76m to 3m as a first step; halving VAT on food; capping electricity prices at 50 øre/kWh; cutting fuel road-use taxes; financed partly by ~NOK 15bn additional oil-fund spending and over NOK 11bn in cuts to immigration/integration spending, foreign aid, and bureaucracy ([FrP alternative budget 2025 PDF](https://www.frp.no/files/Statsbudsjett/alt-bud-2025-web.pdf); [Altinget](https://www.altinget.no/artikkel/frps-alternative-budsjett-oeker-oljepengebruk-med-15-milliarder-fjerner-ikke-hele-formuesskatten); [NRK](https://www.nrk.no/norge/frp-varslar-titals-milliardar-i-skatte--og-avgiftskutt-1.17087788)).
- Healthcare: free choice of provider, "money follows the patient," purchase of private capacity to cut queues ([NRK Partiguiden 2025](https://www.nrk.no/valg/2025/partiguide/nb/parti/FRP)).
- **EU/EEA**: under Listhaug the party became an unambiguous "no" party on EU membership and opposes a new referendum — Norway needs "unity and implementation power, not a divisive and polarizing EU debate." FrP clashed openly with Høyre when the latter's 2025 convention resolved to work actively for membership; FrP has signalled willingness to renegotiate parts of the EEA relationship while keeping market access, prompting Solberg's warning not to "tamper with the EEA agreement" ([Dagsavisen, 28 Apr 2023](https://www.dagsavisen.no/nyheter/innenriks/2023/04/28/sylvi-listhaug-med-klar-eu-beskjed-til-hoyre/); [Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/nyheter/hoyre-vedtok-a-jobbe-aktivt-for-norsk-eu-medlemskap-frp-raser/s/5-95-2350132); [Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/nyheter/erna-solberg-advarer-frp-ikke-rot-med-eos-avtalen/s/5-95-2350884)).

### 5.2 Anti-media and anti-"woke" politics

Two fronts matter for the party's populist profile:

- **Public broadcasting and press subsidies**: FrP's alternative 2025 budget proposed cutting NRK's appropriation by NOK 3bn (of ~7.5bn) — halving NRK within a year — abolishing press subsidies, and ultimately privatizing, splitting and selling NRK (privatization is in the party program) ([Dagsavisen, 15 Apr 2025](https://www.dagsavisen.no/nyheter/innenriks/2025/04/15/frp-vil-kutte-pressestotte-og-selge-nrk/); [Altinget](https://www.altinget.no/lovebakken/artikkel/frp-foreslaar-radikale-endringer-vil-kutte-halve-nrk-paa-ett-aar)).
- **Anti-woke resolutions**: the party convention passed a resolution against "woke"; commentators counter that FrP itself practices identity politics for young men — "male identity politics as the party's new success formula" ([VG, Hans Petter Sjøli](https://www.vg.no/nyheter/i/4BRVxq/hans-petter-sjoeli-frp-bedriver-identitetspolitikk); [Civita](https://civita.no/politikk-og-samfunn/norsk-politikk/listhaugs-nygamle-frp-er-venstresidens-vate-drom/)).

### 5.3 The populist register: "folk flest" versus "eliten"

FrP's historic slogan is *"For folk flest"* ("For ordinary people"), and Norwegian political science characterizes its rhetoric through the "folk flest versus eliten" opposition ([Norsk statsvitenskapelig tidsskrift 1/2021](https://www.idunn.no/nst/2021/01/populisme_mot_elitisme)). Listhaug's 2018 book *Der andre tier* ("Where Others Stay Silent", Kagge) is the key rhetorical document: an attack on the "Oslo elite," national media, KrF, Støre and the EU, containing lines such as *"Vi deler ut oppholdstillatelser og statsborgerskap som om det skulle være drops"* ("We hand out residence permits and citizenships as if they were candy") ([Ark.no](https://www.ark.no/produkt/boker/dokumentar-og-faktaboker/der-andre-tier-9788248923558); [Dagbladet review](https://www.dagbladet.no/kultur/listhaugs-manifest-er-tynn-politisk-suppe/70348329)). Analysts such as Kristin Clemet (Civita) describe her style as classic populist "folk og elite" framing — "us versus them rather than position versus position" ([Nettavisen/Clemet](https://www.nettavisen.no/meninger/friskemeninger/ja-populisme-kan-vare-farlig/s/12-95-3423534261)).

Representative convention rhetoric, 2024–25: it is "no longer a given that our children will have it better"; Norway risks becoming a **"gangster's paradise"**; and — fusing the anti-elite and nativist registers — *"Why should we stay silent for forces that call us right-wing extremists, because we demand that people who come to Norway learn Norwegian, follow Norwegian law and adapt to our values?"* ([VG](https://www.vg.no/nyheter/i/1M9PMW/listhaug-til-frp-landsmoetet-bekymret-for-at-norge-er-over-toppen); [VG](https://www.vg.no/nyheter/i/rr8yrl/sylvi-listhaug-taler-til-frps-landsmoete-hele-landet-skal-faa-oppleve-mer-frp-politikk)).

Two further rhetorical episodes bracket the period. April 2022: Listhaug told those who have a problem with Koran burnings to *"flytt til et annet land"* ("move to another country") ([Vårt Land, 29 Apr 2022](https://www.vl.no/nyheter/2022/04/29/frp-listhaug-til-de-som-har-problemer-med-koran-brenning-flytt-til-et-annet-land/)). And on Israel/Gaza, the party moved from refusing to condemn Israel's blockade in October 2023 (Listhaug: Hamas bears responsibility for the suffering) to Listhaug's August 2025 debate concession that "Israel has gone too far in Gaza" and that full occupation of Gaza would be *"helt uaktuelt"* (completely unacceptable) ([NRK](https://www.nrk.no/nyheter/frp-vil-ikke-fordomme-israels-blokade-1.16598462); [Nationen](https://www.nationen.no/listhaug-israel-har-gatt-for-langt-i-gaza/s/5-148-769305); [TV 2](https://www.tv2.no/nyheter/listhaug-om-israels-gaza-plan-helt-uaktuelt/17432116/)).

## 6. Nativism and immigration politics

### 6.1 Programmatic radicalization, 2021–2029

The 2021–2025 program already "significantly tightened" asylum policy under the umbrella frame of "sustainable immigration" ([NRK Partiguiden 2021](https://www.nrk.no/valg/2021/partiguiden/nb/tema/innvandring/)). The 2025–2029 program goes much further and proposes the most radical asylum overhaul of any Norwegian parliamentary party:

1. **Abolition of border asylum.** FrP wants to "stop the possibility of obtaining residence in Norway by seeking asylum at the border"; the party's own headline is *"Null asylinnvandring til Norge"* ("Zero asylum immigration to Norway"), with "all protection provided in neighboring areas" (*nærområdene*) ([FrP: "Null asylinnvandring til Norge"](https://www.frp.no/nyheter/null-asylinnvandring-til-norge); [FrP: "Et nytt asylsystem"](https://www.frp.no/var-politikk/innvandring-og-integrering/asylordningen); analysis by University of Oslo criminologists: [IKRS/UiO](https://www.jus.uio.no/ikrs/forskning/prosjekter/beyond/aktuelle-saker/partienes-debatt-om-neromradene.html)).
2. **Third-country processing** (Rwanda/Denmark-style): asylum seekers arriving in Norway sent to centers outside Europe; if protection is granted, residence is granted *in the third country*, not Norway ([Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/asylpolitikk/fremskrittspartiet/norsk-politikk/frp-vil-sende-asylsokere-til-tredjeland-og-kutte-kraftig-inn-pa-familiegjenforening/s/5-95-2136403)). In April 2024 Høyre and FrP reached a parliamentary agreement pointing toward third-country processing and fewer protections ([Vårt Land, 3 Apr 2024](https://www.vl.no/nyheter/2024/04/03/h-og-frp-faerre-skal-fa-beskyttelse-i-norge-faerre-skal-fa-samle-familien/)).
3. **Stop to UN quota refugees**, including a parliamentary motion to suspend quota-refugee intake for as long as the Ukraine war lasts ([Stortinget](https://www.stortinget.no/no/Saker-og-publikasjoner/Saker/Sak/?p=96112); [FrP](https://www.frp.no/var-politikk/innvandring-og-integrering/flyktninger)).
4. **Family-reunification cuts**: limits to spouses over 24 and children under 15, documented Norwegian-language skills and self-sufficiency requirements; no family reunification for unaccompanied minors ([FrP immigration committee report, 57 proposals](https://www.frp.no/files/Innvandring/InnvandringsogIntegreringsutvalget_Ny.pdf)).
5. **Citizenship tightening** (fronted by Erlend Wiborg, 2025): minimum 10 years' residence, B2-level language test, a test in "Norwegian and fundamental universal values," a Danish-style loyalty oath, self-sufficiency requirements, and a permanent citizenship bar for anyone sentenced to more than 90 days' unconditional imprisonment ([Utrop](https://www.utrop.no/nyheter/nytt/378250/); [Nettavisen/Wiborg](https://www.nettavisen.no/norsk-debatt/nye-krav-til-statsborgerskap-dette-foreslar-frp/o/5-95-3007218)).
6. **"Integration contract" and settlement caps**: all "non-Western" immigrants to sign a contract accepting "Norwegian laws, rules and values"; a settlement stop for refugees in municipalities where non-Western immigrants exceed 15% of the population ([VG](https://www.vg.no/nyheter/i/LMWdX4/frp-foreslaar-integreringskontrakt); [FrP](https://www.frp.no/nyheter/en-mer-baerekraftig-innvandrings-og-bosettingspolitikk)).
7. **Deportation reform** (*utvisningsreform*, presented 8 January 2026 by Listhaug and Wiborg, Danish-inspired): all foreigners committing serious crime expelled "regardless of connection to Norway"; removal of the proportionality assessment for those with family in Norway; loss of citizenship for dual citizens convicted of serious crimes; weakening of refugees' absolute protection against expulsion. A Storting majority voted the proposal down around 30 April 2026 ([Stortinget](https://www.stortinget.no/no/Saker-og-publikasjoner/Saker/Sak/?p=106246); [Utrop](https://www.utrop.no/nyheter/notis/376739/); [Aftenposten op-ed by Wiborg and Listhaug: "Frp fikk rett. Nå haster det."](https://www.aftenposten.no/meninger/debatt/i/n19ORL/erlend-wiborg-og-sylvi-listhaug-frp-fikk-rett-naa-haster-det)).

**Ukrainian refugees.** FrP supported but pushed beyond the Støre government's 2024 tightening of collective protection for Ukrainians: Listhaug argued Ukrainians from "relatively safe areas" should not receive automatic protection; FrP wanted roughly 5,000 Ukrainians already in Norway returned to safe areas and demanded the return of young Ukrainian men of fighting age ("Norway cannot be a haven for deserters"), and proposed cutting benefits to Ukrainian refugees ([Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/nyheter/frp-reagerer-etter-regjering-forslag-ukrainere-fra-trygge-omrader-ma-reise-hjem/s/5-95-2040200); [Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/nyheter/frp-vil-sende-5000-ukrainere-tilbake-til-trygge-omrader-i-ukraina/s/5-95-2139981); [Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/nyheter/unge-ukrainske-menn-strommer-til-norge-frp-vil-kaste-dem-ut/s/5-95-2650838); government policy: [regjeringen.no](https://www.regjeringen.no/en/whats-new/the-government-is-changing-the-rules-norway-will-grant-collective-protection-to-fewer-ukrainians/id3055194/)).

### 6.2 "Snikislamisering" and anti-Islam rhetoric

The term *snikislamisering* ("sneak/stealth Islamization") was coined by Siv Jensen in 2009. Listhaug revived and defended it in summer 2021:

- July 2021: *"Snikislamisering er ingen konspirasjonsteori. Det handler om særkrav fra muslimer som vil ha endringer i samfunnet vårt"* ("Sneak Islamization is no conspiracy theory. It is about special demands from Muslims who want changes in our society") — citing gender-segregated swimming lessons and hijab in police uniforms ([NRK, July 2021](https://www.nrk.no/norge/listhaug-forsvarer-bruken-av-ordet-snikislamisering-1.15590047)).
- August 2021, Oslo Symposium: sneak Islamization "has only gotten worse" since 2009; "there are Islamists who fight to see mosques on every street corner" ([Vårt Land, 20 Aug 2021](https://www.vl.no/nyheter/2021/08/20/listhaug-blaser-liv-i-debatt-om-snikislamisering-og-tar-nadelost-oppgjor-med-krf/)). She insisted FrP has "nothing to be ashamed of" after "12 years of snikislamisering" and has never apologized for the word ([Aftenposten](https://www.aftenposten.no/norge/politikk/i/w8z1RG/12-aar-med-snikislamisering-listhaug-mener-frp-har-ingenting-aa-skamme-seg-over)).
- Critics: conspiracy-theory researchers argued the term implies a hidden Muslim plot and originates in far-right Eurabia conspiracy discourse ([Dagsavisen, 4 Aug 2021](https://www.dagsavisen.no/kultur/boker/2021/08/04/konspirasjonsekspert-listhaug-bor-slutte-a-bruke-ordet-snikislamisering/)); extremism researcher Lars Gule accused Listhaug of claiming definitional power over the term ([Resett, 5 Aug 2021](https://resett.no/2021/08/05/lars-gule-listhaug-krever-definisjonsmakt-av-snikislamisering/)).
- The crucial scholarly datum: C-REX's analysis "The Progress Party's anti-Islam hinterland" (Jupskås-affiliated, 2021), based on interviews with members of SIAN (Stop Islamisation of Norway) Facebook groups, found almost all voted FrP; it calls Listhaug's "conspiratorial term 'sneak Islamization'" "a prime example of right-wing populist rhetoric" and notes the same narrative was central to the 22 July terrorist's worldview: "in these circles, people listen more to Sylvi Listhaug than to most other politicians" ([C-REX/UiO](https://www.sv.uio.no/c-rex/english/news-and-events/right-now/2021/the-progess-party%E2%80%99s-anti-islam-hinterland.html); [ScienceNorway](https://www.sciencenorway.no/blog-ideology-media/the-progress-partys-anti-islam-hinterland/1898683)).

Listhaug also favors banning hijab in elementary schools and full-face veils, framed as fighting "the culture of social control" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvi_Listhaug)). Notably, a 2025-era retrospective indicates she has *softened or relativized* some of this vocabulary as leader, and her 2025 campaign featured the "twist" that "most immigrants contribute" — group-specific criticism retained within an inclusive-sounding frame ([Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/nyheter/svenske-tilstander-snikislamisering-og-imamsleiking-dette-sier-listhaug-na/s/12-95-3424091305); [Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/norsk-debatt/sylvi-listhaugs-valgkamp-twist-innvandrere-kan-sikre-frp-seier/o/5-95-2252271)).

### 6.3 Welfare chauvinism and the cost-accounting frame

FrP's nativism is articulated primarily through *fiscal sustainability* rather than ethno-cultural purity. The party demands an annual public **"innvandringsregnskap"** (immigration account) from SSB showing immigrants' net fiscal contributions by country of origin and immigration category, modeled on the Brochmann commission and Danish practice ([FrP](https://www.frp.no/nyheter/innvandringsregnskap-er-avgjorende-for-integreringen); [Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/okonomi/frp-vil-ha-et-offentlig-innvandringsregnskap-i-norge/s/5-95-2439712)), and argues current immigration "is not sustainable for the welfare society long-term" ([FrP committee report](https://www.frp.no/files/Innvandring/InnvandringsogIntegreringsutvalget_Ny.pdf)).

This is the textbook case of **welfare chauvinism** — a concept literally coined on the Norwegian and Danish Progress Parties (Andersen & Bjørklund 1990). Mette Wiggen (Leeds) argues that FrP-style welfare chauvinism has been normalized and mainstreamed across Europe ([Wiggen, IPPR Progressive Review 2022](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/newe.12314)). On the communication side, Bente Kalsnes' work on populist social-media logic shows FrP extracting the most engagement from immigration content on Facebook of any Norwegian party ([Kalsnes, Kristiania](https://www.kristiania.no/en/about-kristiania/employees/school-of-communication-leadership-and-marketing/departement-of-communication/bente-kalsnes/); [Kristiania Valganalyse 2023](https://www.kristiania.no/forskning/forskningsgrupper/polcom/Valganalyse/Valganalyse2023/auliejacobsen/)).

### 6.4 Incidents and reactions, 2021–2026

- **The 25 June 2022 Oslo Pride shooting** (two killed, 21+ wounded; classified as Islamist terrorism; perpetrator Zaniar Matapour convicted): Listhaug condemned the attack, announced she would walk in the Pride parade, and demanded in a TV 2 op-ed that Muslim faith communities confront homophobia. The queer-Muslim organization Salam and the LGBT outlet Blikk rejected her participation, citing her record on Pride and same-sex marriage: "Thanks, but no thanks, Listhaug" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Oslo_shooting); [Blikk](https://www.blikk.no/fremskrittspartiet-frp-islamofobi/takk-men-nei-takk-listhaug-vi-vil-helst-ikke-se-deg-i-prideparaden/221551); [Utrop](https://www.utrop.no/nyheter/nytt/312013/)). Her policy emphasis afterwards was deportation/entry bans for foreign citizens behind "illegal actions" ([Civicus Monitor](https://monitor.civicus.org/explore/shooting-oslo-pride-reopens-concerns-over-lgbtqi-rights-climate-activists-arrested-government-intervenes-oil-strike/)).
- **The Islamic Council Norway (IRN)** has repeatedly accused Listhaug of creating "fertile ground for Muslim-hatred" (*grobunn for muslimhat*), e.g., when she linked COVID infection rates to poor integration ([Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/okonomi/listhaug-kobler-koronasmitte-til-darlig-integrering/s/12-95-3424016081)).
- **2025 campaign**: an FrP campaign video showed an ethnic-Norwegian boy victimized by a masked, multicultural gang; Umar Ashraf, director of the Antirasistisk Senter, said FrP "profits from making people afraid" and that the rhetoric frightens minority youth into silence ([Avisa Oslo](https://www.ao.no/unge-med-minoritetsbakgrunn-som-har-blitt-offer-for-hoyresidens-valgkamp-frp-tjener-pa-a-gjore-folk-redde/o/5-128-1138427)).
- **The adviser racism scandal (May 2026)**: on 2 May 2026 TV 2 published secret recordings of party adviser Hårek Hansen — made in an Oslo bar the evening before FrP's national convention — calling Norwegian-Pakistanis *"minusvarianter"* ("minus variants") who "preferably shouldn't have more children." PM Støre called the remarks *"reinspikka rasistiske"* ("out-and-out racist") and criticized Listhaug's initial silence; Listhaug then stated: *"Dette er en rasistisk ytring som vi tar sterk avstand fra"* ("This is a racist utterance from which we strongly distance ourselves"). Hansen lost his job and party membership. A parallel press-ethics debate erupted over TV 2's hidden-recording methods ([TV 2, 2 May 2026](https://www.tv2.no/nyheter/frp-radgiver-pakistanere-bor-helst-ikke-fa-flere-barn/18803136/); [VG/Støre](https://www.vg.no/nyheter/i/q6O4Em/stoere-raser-reinspikka-rasistiske-uttalelser); [Aftenposten](https://www.aftenposten.no/norge/politikk/i/6qPJVe/frp-raadgiver-skal-ha-kalt-pakistanere-minusvarianter-for-en-forferdelig-ting-aa-tenke); [Finansavisen, 3 May 2026](https://www.finansavisen.no/politikk/2026/05/03/8348274/tar-avstand-fra-harek-hansens-uttalelser-ikke-rom-for-i-frp)). The episode illustrates both the persistence of ethno-nationalist currents at the party's edges and the leadership's active boundary-policing against them.

## 7. Crime and law-and-order politics: "Swedish conditions"

### 7.1 Program and proposals

FrP's justice program 2021–2026, culminating in the 2025–2029 program and the August 2025 campaign package, includes:

- **Lowering the criminal age of responsibility from 15 to 14**, with "reinforced closed child-welfare institutions" for criminal children below that age (opposed publicly by the Children's Ombud) ([frp.no](https://www.frp.no/nyhetsdogn/vil-senke-den-kriminelle-lavalderen-til-14-ar); [NRK](https://www.nrk.no/sorlandet/frp-vil-senke-den-kriminelle-lavalderen-_-barneombudet-er-uenig-1.16510146)).
- **Raising the maximum penalty for the most serious crimes to 50 years** (from 21/30) ([NRK, Aug 2025](https://www.nrk.no/nyheter/fremskrittspartiet-vil-oke-maksstraffen-for-alvorlige-forbrytelser-til-50-ar-1.17520912); [Aftenposten](https://www.aftenposten.no/norge/politikk/i/VzEgel/frp-vil-bevaepne-tollvesenet-og-oeke-maksstraff-til-50-aar-galskap)).
- Stricter penalties for repeat offenders, separate youth prisons, GPS ankle monitors for young repeat offenders ([frp.no gang-crime page](https://www.frp.no/var-politikk/justis-og-beredskap/gjengkriminalitet)).
- Roughly 800 additional police positions and expanded remand/prison capacity in the alternative 2025 budget ([FrP alternative budget](https://www.frp.no/files/Statsbudsjett/alt-bud-2025-web.pdf)).
- The **"Ta tryggheten tilbake"** ("Take back security") election package, launched by Listhaug and Limi on ~7–8 August 2025 at the symbolically chosen Svinesund border crossing with Sweden: twelve measures including arming the customs service, facial/object recognition at the border, increased youth-prison capacity, and the 50-year maximum ([TV 2](https://www.tv2.no/nyheter/slik-vil-frp-ta-tryggheten-tilbake/17979320/)).
- The recurring 13-point gang-crime package (Dok 8 proposals 2023–25): **visitasjonssoner** (Danish-style stop-and-search zones where police can search for weapons without individual suspicion in time-limited periods), **doubled sentencing frames for gang-related crime** and for crimes committed in designated gang-affected areas, sharply higher penalties for illegal weapons possession, curfews for convicted gang criminals, closed institutions for under-18 gang offenders, and criminalizing the recruitment of children into crime ([frp.no](https://www.frp.no/nyheter/dobbel-straff-er-et-viktig-tiltak-mot-gjengkriminalitet); [Stortinget Innst. 133 S (2024–2025)](https://www.stortinget.no/no/Saker-og-publikasjoner/Publikasjoner/Innstillinger/Stortinget/2024-2025/inns-202425-133s/?all=true)).

**The February 2025 parliamentary victory.** A non-socialist majority (Høyre, FrP, Sp, with KrF securing the majority on visitation zones) defeated the Støre government, adopting resolutions instructing it to: present visitation-zone legislation by 1 September 2025, implement a fast-track court process for offenders under 18, sharpen penalties for illegal weapons possession, and prepare doubled sentencing frames for gang crime. The new justice minister Astri Aas-Hansen (Ap) opposed the proposals on her first working day and was overruled ([Dagsavisen, 11 Feb 2025](https://www.dagsavisen.no/nyheter/innenriks/2025/02/11/smell-for-store-regjeringen-i-stortinget-blir-overkjort-pa-kriminalitet/); [VG](https://www.vg.no/nyheter/i/xmz8B8/stortingssmell-for-stoere-krf-sikrer-flertall-for-visitasjonssoner); [ABC Nyheter, 20 Feb 2025](https://www.abcnyheter.no/nyheter/norge/2025/02/20/196068074/stortinget-vedtok-visitasjonssoner-nok-et-nederlag-for-regjeringen)). This is the clearest case in the period of FrP converting opposition agenda-setting into binding policy instruction.

**Prisons.** The factual anchor for FrP's "rent prison places abroad" position is the 2015–2018 Norgerhaven agreement with the Netherlands, signed by FrP justice minister Anders Anundsen (242 places; 936 convicts transferred by February 2018, ~80% foreign nationals; Listhaug called it "worth every krone"; terminated 2018 once the sentencing queue was eliminated) ([Dagbladet, Feb 2018](https://www.dagbladet.no/nyheter/regjeringen-avslutter-fengselsavtale-i-nederland/69540772)). In 2021–2026 the emphasis shifted to deportation and serving sentences in home countries (*soningsoverføring*).

### 7.2 The "svenske tilstander" discourse

FrP has used "Swedish conditions" as a warning label for years — defined by Listhaug as the use of very young people for serious crime, violence as a commodity (*"vold som handelsvare"*), and grenades in public spaces. FrP claimed Swedish criminal networks had established themselves in all twelve Norwegian police districts under the Støre government, invoking Kripos warnings ([frp.no](https://www.frp.no/nyheter/svenske-tilstander-rykker-stadig-naermere)).

**The pivotal event** came two weeks after the election: on **23 September 2025 a hand grenade exploded in Pilestredet near Bislett in central Oslo**; 13-year-old boys were apprehended (one reportedly paid NOK 30,000 for the job), and police investigated links to the Swedish Foxtrot network. Listhaug declared: *"Når militære håndgranater går av midt i Oslo sentrum og så unge som 13-åringer kan være involvert, er likhetene med utviklingen i Sverige slående. Nå er vi der Fremskrittspartiet lenge har advart mot – vi har fått svenske tilstander i Norge."* ("When military hand grenades go off in the middle of central Oslo and children as young as 13 may be involved, the similarities with the development in Sweden are striking. We are now where the Progress Party has long warned — we have got Swedish conditions in Norway.") ([NRK](https://www.nrk.no/stor-oslo/to-13-aringer-innbrakt-etter-eksplosjon-pa-bislett-1.17583345); [TV 2](https://www.tv2.no/nyheter/innenriks/listhaug-vi-har-fatt-svenske-tilstander-i-norge/18130371/)). A coda relevant to the framing contest: the criminal cases against the 13-year-olds (below the age of criminal responsibility) were dropped in February 2026 ([VG](https://www.vg.no/nyheter/i/aJbWPA/granatsaken-i-pilestredet-saken-mot-en-av-13-aaringene-er-henlagt)).

**The discursive duel.** Støre repeatedly inverted the frame: after Listhaug's campaign visit to Rinkeby in Sweden he said *"alle skjønner at hun er der for å skremme. Vi har ingen Rinkeby i Norge"* ("everyone understands she is there to frighten people. We have no Rinkeby in Norway"); in the 2023 throne-speech debate he quipped that she sounded like she was "more in a Swedish Riksdag debate than in the Storting"; and he charged: *"Jeg mener Sylvi Listhaug er med på å skape svenske tilstander"* ("I believe Sylvi Listhaug is helping to create Swedish conditions") — arguing FrP's integration and welfare cuts produce the marginalization that breeds gangs. SV leader Kirsti Bergstø made the same inversion ([Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/nyheter/tre-ganger-store-har-avvist-listhaugs-advarsler/s/5-95-2658061); [VG](https://www.vg.no/nyheter/i/W0ovEg/stoere-frp-vil-skape-svenske-tilstander-i-norge)). In the October 2025 throne-speech debate Støre countered: "I am for solving *Norwegian* conditions. We have youth crime that fell for many years — and rose on the Progress Party's watch" ([Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/nyheter/frp-leder-sylvi-listhaug-griller-store/s/5-95-2670758)).

**Meta-debate.** In October 2025 NRK's language chief warned journalists against using "svenske tilstander," calling it "politically charged, imprecise and burdensome for Swedes in Norway" — itself triggering accusations that NRK was shielding the government ([Aftenposten](https://www.aftenposten.no/norge/i/63pB9O/nrks-spraaksjef-advarer-journalister-om-aa-bruke-uttrykket-svenske-tilstander)). The Police Federation meanwhile titled a piece "Swedish conditions are becoming Nordic conditions" ([Politiets Fellesforbund](https://pf.no/aktuelt/medlemmer/svenske-tilstander-er-i-ferd-med-a-bli-nordiske-tilstander)).

### 7.3 Immigrant-crime framing and the statistics battles

- **The December 2024 SSB tables.** FrP's parliamentary group paid SSB for detailed tables on charged persons by country background (first in 2019, repeated for 2020–2023 data, published ~9 December 2024): nearly 130 charges (*siktelser*) per 1,000 residents among immigrants and Norwegian-born to immigrant parents versus just over 80 per 1,000 in the rest of the population (~60% overrepresentation, unadjusted), with overrepresentation *higher* in 2020–23 than 2015–18 ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/sosiale-forhold-og-kriminalitet/kriminalitet-og-rettsvesen/statistikk/etterforskede-lovbrudd/artikler/siktelser-og-siktede-personer-etter-innvandringsbakgrunn); [Dagsavisen/NTB, 9 Dec 2024](https://www.dagsavisen.no/nyheter/innenriks/2024/12/09/ssb-langt-flere-siktelser-mot-innvandrere-enn-mot-ovrig-befolkning/)). The most-cited figure: young Somali-background men in Oslo averaged more than two charges per person (2,119.8 per 1,000) over 2020–2023 — though 271 charged individuals accounted for 3,290 charges, i.e., heavy concentration among repeat offenders ([NRK](https://www.nrk.no/stor-oslo/kriminalitet-og-innvandring_-unge-somaliske-menn-topper-listen-i-oslo-1.17161405)). Listhaug called the figures "dramatic" and demanded more police, stricter youth sentences and a lower bar for deporting foreign repeat offenders ([frp.no](https://www.frp.no/nyheter/nye-tall-viser-at-enkelte-innvandrergrupper-star-for-svaert-mye-kriminalitet)). SSB cautioned that the tables count charges, not persons, and are unadjusted for age, sex or socioeconomics.
- **"Hidden information" complaints**: FrP accused the government of burying immigrant-background data in a commissioned expert report on youth crime; the party's framing was explicit: *"Klart det handler om innvandring"* ("Of course it is about immigration") ([Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/ungdomskriminalitet/frp/innvandring/viktig-informasjon-gjemmes-bort-i-rapport-om-ungdomskriminalitet-mener-fremskrittspartiet/s/5-95-2363421); [frp.no](https://www.frp.no/nyheter/ungdomskriminaliteten-klart-det-handler-om-innvandring)). FrP also demanded annual Danish-style published crime statistics by background and a national gang-member register ([Stortinget Innst. 285 S (2024–2025)](https://www.stortinget.no/no/Saker-og-publikasjoner/Publikasjoner/Innstillinger/Stortinget/2024-2025/inns-202425-285s/?all=true)).

### 7.4 What the statistics show, and the critiques

- **Police annual figures 2024** (published 13 Feb 2025): registered crime overall did not increase, but police flagged concern about youth crime and criminal networks ([Politiet](https://www.politiet.no/aktuelt-tall-og-fakta/aktuelt/nyheter/2025/02/13/arstall-2024-registrert-kriminalitet-ikke-okt---men-bekymring-for-ungdomskriminalitet-og-kriminelle-nettverk/)). Oslo police's youth-crime report for 2024 found 185 child/youth repeat offenders versus 217 in 2023 — youth crime "flattening out" but becoming more serious and involving younger children ([Politiet PDF](https://www.politiet.no/globalassets/dokumenter-strategier-og-horinger/oslo/rapporter/barne--og-ungdomskriminalitet/barne--og-ungdomskriminaliteten-i-oslo-2024.pdf)). Kripos identified roughly 400 violence-for-hire assignments targeting youth recruitment by end-2025 ([Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/ungdomskriminalitet/faktaboks/politiets-trusselvurdering/om-ungdomskriminalitet-i-politiets-trusselvurdering-2025/x/5-95-2397248)).
- **Faktisk.no** fact-checks cut both ways. On Listhaug's statistics use (2017, background): "'Immigrants far more criminal,' wrote Listhaug, citing unadjusted figures" — adjustment for age and sex shrinks but does not eliminate overrepresentation ([Faktisk.no](https://www.faktisk.no/artikkel/innvandrere-langt-mer-kriminelle-skrev-listhaug-og-viste-til-ujusterte-tall/120039)). On the August 2025 Listhaug–Aas-Hansen duel: Oslo District Court youth cases doubled (27→54, first halves 2022–2025), but violence reports against minors in Oslo *fell* ~15% year-on-year, and long-term robbery/violence rates per 1,000 residents are lower than ten years ago — both sides could cherry-pick ([Faktisk.no, Sept 2025](https://www.faktisk.no/faktasjekk/har-noreg-meir-ungdomskriminalitet-enn-for/126174)). On Støre's counter-claim that youth crime "shot up" under FrP justice ministers: partially true ([Faktisk.no, Oct 2024](https://www.faktisk.no/artikler/zml6e/skjot-barne-og-ungdomskriminaliteten-i-vaeret-da-frp-hadde-justisminister)).
- **SSB's long-run picture**: immigrants are overrepresented among charged/convicted persons, but the overrepresentation showed a marked downward trend over the 10–15 years to ~2018, with Norwegian-born children of immigrants the most overrepresented group; socioeconomic adjustment reduces gaps. The December 2024 tables showing a 2020–23 *uptick* were a genuine shift that FrP seized on ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/sosiale-forhold-og-kriminalitet/artikler-og-publikasjoner/kriminalitet-og-straff-blant-innvandrere-og-ovrig-befolkning)).
- **Criminological critiques**: University of Oslo criminologists describe a Nordic "punitive turn" — more punishment and control, less welfare-based prevention — and warn it may harm marginalized youth ([forskning.no](https://www.forskning.no/fengsel-kriminalitet/norden-skifter-kurs-mot-gjengkriminalitet-slik-kan-det-ramme-ungdommer/2672167)). Former Director of Public Prosecutions Tor-Aksel Busch said he does not believe Norway will get "Swedish conditions" ([NRK, Sept 2025](https://www.nrk.no/norge/tidligere-riksadvokat-tror-ikke-vi-vil-fa-_svenske-tilstander_-1.17584274)). In April 2026, Swedish criminologist Manne Gerell and journalist Diamant Salihu concluded that Norway has reacted faster and harder than Sweden did at the equivalent stage, making a Swedish trajectory less likely; NRK mapped at least 40 Norwegian criminal cases linked to Swedish networks ([NRK, 28 Apr 2026](https://www.nrk.no/norge/mener-norge-har-reagert-raskere-pa-gjengkriminalitet-enn-sverige-1.17863566)). Center-left critics argue FrP's program of welfare and integration cuts is itself "a recipe for Swedish conditions" ([Nordnorsk debatt](https://www.nordnorskdebatt.no/frps-partiprogram-er-en-oppskrift-pa-svenske-tilstander/o/5-124-336190)).

### 7.5 The Amundsen affair

Per-Willy Amundsen, justice committee chair from October 2021, was among FrP's most aggressive gang-crime voices (accusing police leadership of "manipulating facts"; demanding border controls against Sweden). In March 2024, after Facebook posts and comments around International Women's Day containing derogatory remarks about Arabs, Islam ("Islam har en hel gjeng med psykopater som dreper" — "Islam has a whole gang of psychopaths who kill," per deleted comments) and women, he **resigned as justice committee chair on 10 March 2024** (he blamed whisky and said he could not remember posting). Police opened a hate-speech investigation after four complaints and dismissed the case on 22 March 2024; FrP stripped him of all party rights until 31 January 2025; Helge André Njåstad replaced him as chair. He was later rehabilitated in the party ([Dagsavisen, 10 Mar 2024](https://www.dagsavisen.no/nyheter/innenriks/2024/03/10/per-willy-amundsen-frp-trekker-seg-som-leder-av-justiskomiteen-pa-stortinget/); [VG](https://www.vg.no/nyheter/i/9zjGRp/per-willy-amundsen-fratas-partirettigheter-og-alle-verv-i-frp); [Dagsavisen, 22 Mar 2024](https://www.dagsavisen.no/nyheter/innenriks/2024/03/22/politiet-henlegger-sak-mot-per-willy-amundsen/)).

## 8. Climate change and the energy transition

### 8.1 Programmatic positions

The climate-policy site Energi og Klima summarized FrP's 2021–2025 program as *"Minst mulig, senest mulig"* ("as little as possible, as late as possible") — opposition to the entire architecture of national emission targets and a preference for the lowest possible international commitments ([Energi og Klima, Klimavalg21](https://www.energiogklima.no/meninger-og-analyse/klimavalg21/fremskrittspartiets-klimapolitikk-oppsummert-minst-mulig-senest-mulig)). The 2025–2029 program calls for a "predictable and fact-based climate policy" judged by "costs and effects," stressing that "Norway does not have its own atmosphere" — climate policy should be global, not national ([frp.no Klima page](https://www.frp.no/var-politikk/energi-og-miljo/klima)).

Key positions and parliamentary behavior:

- **Paris targets.** Klassekampen reported (8 January 2025, "Frp vil skrote klimamålet") that FrP has no plans to fulfil the Paris Agreement target: Listhaug repeatedly said FrP only "stands behind" a 40% cut — the pre-2021 target — not the 55%-by-2030 target written into the Climate Act; the party's climate spokesperson said it was "not so important" whether Norway breaks the Paris Agreement ([Klassekampen](https://klassekampen.no/artikkel/2025-01-08/frp-vil-skrote-klimamalet/FAT6) — paywalled; quotes from extracts). Oil Change International's 2025 manifesto analysis found FrP's program "does not mention climate change in any meaningful way in relation to fossil fuel policy," "dismisses international climate obligations," and would "actively roll back environmental regulations" ([OCI](https://oilchange.org/blogs/norwayelection25/)).
- **The 2035 climate white paper.** When the Storting in June 2025 adopted the government's Klimamelding 2035 and wrote the 70–75%-by-2035 target into the Climate Act, FrP supported only one of the committee's eleven recommendations, stating that "the last thing Norway needs is even more unrealistic climate targets with instruments that make everyday life more expensive and further weaken the competitiveness of Norwegian business" ([Innst. 520 S (2024–2025), stortinget.no](https://www.stortinget.no/no/Saker-og-publikasjoner/Publikasjoner/Innstillinger/Stortinget/2024-2025/inns-202425-520s/?all=true)).
- **Repealing the 2030 climate-neutrality goal.** On 26 February 2026 the Storting voted to scrap Norway's 2016 goal of climate neutrality by 2030, on a Senterpartiet proposal (tabled November 2025) carried with the votes of FrP, Høyre, Rødt, KrF and Sp — note the cross-bloc inclusion of Rødt; climate minister Andreas Bjelland Eriksen (Ap) called it "a terrible wrong signal" that "will be noticed internationally." FrP reportedly hoped the decision "would mark the beginning of a new direction in Norwegian climate policy" ([Finansavisen, 26 Feb 2026](https://www.finansavisen.no/politikk/2026/02/26/8331941/stortinget-dropper-norges-mal-om-a-bli-klimanoytralt-innen-2030); [Altinget](https://www.altinget.no/artikkel/stortinget-vedtok-aa-oppheve-klimamaal-det-vil-bli-lagt-merke-til-internasjonalt); [Stortinget case page](https://www.stortinget.no/no/Saker-og-publikasjoner/Saker/Sak/?p=93961)).
- **CO2 tax and ETS2.** FrP opposes the escalating CO2 tax (Listhaug: an *"avgiftssjokk"* — tax shock — by 2035) and argues Norway must not implement the EU's ETS2 (road transport and buildings) on top of the CO2 tax ("double taxation"); the Storting voted to join ETS2 with Sp and FrP against ([Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/nyheter/arbeiderpartiet-og-fremskrittspartiet-i-tottene-pa-hverandre-listhaug-mener-co2-avgift-kan-gi-avgiftssjokk-i-2035/s/5-95-3038181); [frp.no](https://www.frp.no/nyheter/ny-eu-avgift-gir-dyrere-drivstoff)).

### 8.2 Climate skepticism: the tolerated denialist wing

The party's official platform does not deny anthropogenic climate change — it accepts it rhetorically while opposing nearly all national instruments. But it has tolerated open denial from senior figures:

- **Carl I. Hagen**, honorary chairman and MP 2021–2025, is Norway's most prominent political climate denier: a 2017 Aftenposten op-ed disputing IPCC science (climate scientists replied that "Carl I. Hagen spreads untrue claims about climate change"); "All costs to reduce CO2 emissions are wasted" (2019); the claim that IPCC models "have been wrong for 40 years"; "We have no climate crisis" (2021); visits to the denialist group Klimarealistene at Storting expense; CICERO researcher Bjørn H. Samset called his claims *"klovnete"* (clownish) ([Aftenposten](https://www.aftenposten.no/meninger/debatt/i/2734r/carl-i-hagen-sprer-usanne-paastander-om-klimaendringer-og-drivhuseffekt-benestad-og-nordli); [Dagbladet, 2019](https://www.dagbladet.no/nyheter/alle-kostnader-til-a-redusere-co2-utslipp-er-bortkastet/70786706); [snl.no](https://snl.no/Carl_I._Hagen); [Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/na24/klovnete-av-carl-i-hagen/s/12-95-3423293065)).
- **Listhaug** herself said in 2011: *"Det er ikke bevist at menneskeskapte klimagassutslipp fører til klimaendringer"* ("It is not proven that human CO2 emissions lead to climate change. It is primarily an excuse to introduce more taxes and fees") ([VG, 2011: "Ler av klimahysteri"](https://www.vg.no/nyheter/innenriks/i/jR0dA/ler-av-klimahysteri)). As oil minister in 2019 she modified this ("I believe they are also human-caused...") ([Filter Nyheter](https://filternyheter.no/sju-sitater-dette-har-var-nye-oljeminister-uttalt-om-klima/)). Asked in the 2020s whether she regretted the 2011 statements, she answered *"Hva hjelper det å angre?"* ("What good does regretting do?") ([Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/nyheter/sylvi-listhaug-om-omstridt-klimautspill-hva-hjelper-det-a-angre/s/12-95-3423898374)). Her first leader's speech (May 2021) attacked "climate hysteria and billion-kroner waste"; in 2025 she said "left-radical socialists use climate as an excuse" ([VG](https://www.vg.no/nyheter/innenriks/i/we1JM4/listhaug-til-frps-landsmoete-ut-mot-klimahysteri-og-mdg); [Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/nyheter/listhaug-venstreradikale-sosialister-bruker-klima-som-unnskyldning/s/12-95-3424164139)).

### 8.3 Oil and gas: the most pro-petroleum party

- FrP supports opening new areas "as quickly as possible," numbered licensing rounds and a 100-year industrial perspective; it explicitly rejects any end date for petroleum — "demand must determine whether we stop in 100, 200 or 300 years" ([frp.no petroleum page](https://www.frp.no/var-politikk/energi-og-miljo/petroleumsvirksomhet); [VG](https://www.vg.no/nyheter/i/93n38M/stoere-advarer-mot-at-frps-politikk-vil-foere-til-sylvistopp-for-oljenaeringen-faar-takk-tilbake)). Listhaug frames Norwegian oil and gas as "part of the solution" to the climate problem ([Aftenposten](https://www.aftenposten.no/norge/i/GG2xmV/klimaorganisasjoner-ut-mot-listhaug-staar-for-en-gammeldags-faktafornektende-klimapolitikk)).
- **Electrification of the shelf**: FrP proposed halting all further electrification of offshore platforms with power from land, calling it "expensive symbolic policy"; in the 2025 campaign it promised a "full stop" ([TU.no](https://www.tu.no/artikler/frp-foreslar-a-stoppe-videre-elektrifisering-av-norsk-sokkel/522338); [Finansavisen, 26 Aug 2025](https://www.finansavisen.no/samfunn/2025/08/26/8288279/frp-lover-full-stans-i-elektrifisering-av-oljefelt)).
- **LoVeSe**: FrP wants impact assessments (the legal precursor to opening) of Lofoten–Vesterålen–Senja, the Barents Sea north, the Møre blocks and Jan Mayen; in March 2026 NRK reported FrP and Høyre "open for oil drilling in Lofoten and Vesterålen," against 71% local opposition in a 2025 survey ([NRK Nordland, 2026](https://www.nrk.no/nordland/frp-og-hogre-opnar-for-oljeboring-i-lofoten-og-vesteralen-_-auf-reagerer-1.17811203)). Post-election, FrP helped secure a Storting majority pressure for the 26th licensing round ([Altinget](https://www.altinget.no/artikkel/tybring-gjedde-sikrer-trolig-flertall-for-aa-lyse-ut-26-konsesjonsrunde)).

### 8.4 Wind, cables, electricity prices, nuclear

- **Onshore wind**: no in principle, allowed only after a local referendum; all direct and indirect wind subsidies to be removed ([frp.no](https://www.frp.no/var-politikk/energi-og-miljo/vindkraft)). Listhaug campaigns against wind-power "littering" of Norwegian nature.
- **Offshore wind**: no to subsidizing floating offshore wind; attacks on the ~NOK 35bn support framework for Sørlige Nordsjø II. In June 2026 FrP supplied the decisive votes for Høyre's proposal to pause the floating offshore-wind project Utsira Nord pending external quality assurance, against protests from LO, NHO and Equinor ([frp.no](https://www.frp.no/nyheter/frp-sier-nei-til-a-subsidiere-flytende-havvind); [TU.no](https://www.tu.no/artikler/frp-sikrer-flertall-for-hoyres-havvindforslag-vekker-harme/573244)).
- **Electricity prices and cables**: during the 2021–2023 electricity-price crisis FrP branded high prices a "politician-made crisis," ran a *"Stopp strømranet"* ("stop the electricity robbery") campaign, demanded a maximum consumer price of 0.50 kr/kWh (voted down), and demanded export controls tied to reservoir levels; it now opposes new interconnectors to Europe ([frp.no](https://www.frp.no/nyheter/frp-forslag-om-makspris-fikk-ikke-flertall); [Fornybar Norge party comparison, 2025](https://www.fornybarnorge.no/nyheter/2025/viktige-veivalg-i-energipolitikken/)). An important nuance: FrP's own energy minister Tord Lien granted the concessions for the NordLink and North Sea Link cables in 2014 — the anti-cable line is a post-2021 shift ([Rødt blog on NorthConnect](https://roedt.no/blogg/utenlandskabler-en-lang-og-seig-kamp-mot-northconnect)).
- **Nuclear**: FrP has become the loudest pro-nuclear party, with a comprehensive 2024–25 Storting package (Dok 8:113 S (2024–2025)): Nordic nuclear cooperation, a dedicated nuclear authority, Euratom association, siting studies. Energy spokesman Tor Mikkel Wara: "We must stop hesitating. This is about electricity prices, jobs and competitiveness" ([Stortinget](https://www.stortinget.no/no/Saker-og-publikasjoner/Publikasjoner/Representantforslag/2024-2025/dok8-202425-113s/?all=true); [frp.no](https://www.frp.no/nyheter/ja-til-kjernekraftsatsing-i-norge)).

### 8.5 Transport: the EV paradox and toll populism

Norway's celebrated EV transition largely happened while FrP held the transport ministry (Ketil Solvik-Olsen, 2013–2018) and the finance ministry, which preserved the VAT and registration-tax exemptions ([The Drive](https://www.thedrive.com/tech/12323/the-secret-behind-norways-ev-miracle-isnt-oil); [Undark, 9 June 2025](https://undark.org/2025/06/09/norway-electric-cars/)). FrP's preferred "equalization," however, is downward: cutting ICE-car taxes to EV levels rather than taxing EVs up — with the exception of the 2019 toll crisis, when FrP proposed full VAT on EVs to cut tolls ([ABC Nyheter, 2 Oct 2019](https://www.abcnyheter.no/motor/bil/2019/10/02/195615005/hoyre-vil-snakke-om-veiprising-frp-om-elbil-moms); [Dagbladet, June 2019](https://www.dagbladet.no/nyheter/slik-vil-frp-lose-bompengekrisa-tar-milliarder-fra-elbilistene/71114572)). The 2019 *bompengeopprør* (toll revolt) nearly split FrP out of the Solberg government and remains core party identity; scholars use it as a case of right-wing populist climate-policy opposition (["Right-wing populism and climate policies: Explaining opposition to road tolls in Norway," *Transportation Research Part D*, 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1361920922000529)). FrP continues to campaign on large cuts to road-use and CO2 components of fuel taxes ([frp.no](https://www.frp.no/nyheter/avgiftskutt-for-folk-flest)).

### 8.6 The 2025 election and aftermath

Energy was central to the 2025 campaign: Sp had left the government in early 2025 over EU energy rules, electricity prices dominated voter concerns, and FrP's "cheap energy" package (stop shelf electrification, no new cables, export limits, nuclear, fuel-tax cuts) was credited with much of its surge ([Energy Voice](https://www.energyvoice.com/oilandgas/norway/579671/norway-election-energy-policy-power-exports-coalition/)). The Green European Journal called the election "a fossil consensus," warning that FrP's program "would not only block progress, but actively reverse existing climate measures" ([GEJ, 2025](https://www.greeneuropeanjournal.eu/norways-elections-a-fossil-consensus/)). Post-election, FrP's parliamentary weight translated into the repeal of the 2030 climate-neutrality goal (February 2026), the offshore-wind pause (June 2026), and pressure for new licensing rounds and LoVeSe assessments.

### 8.7 Scholarly framing

- **Krange, Kaltenborn & Hultman, "Cool dudes in Norway: climate change denial among conservative Norwegian men," *Environmental Sociology* 5(1) (2019)**: 63% of conservative Norwegian men deny anthropogenic climate change versus 36% of the rest of the population; denial merges with "xenosceptic" right-wing nationalism — the demographic core of FrP's electorate ([Taylor & Francis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/23251042.2018.1488516)).
- **CICERO's Håkon Sælen** has shown with data from Norway, the US, Germany and Australia that climate attitudes are *more* decisive for right-wing populist support than views on gender, crime or the economy ([ScienceNorway](https://www.sciencenorway.no/climate-environment-politics/why-are-right-wing-populists-so-opposed-to-climate-policy/2507190)).
- The broader far-right literature traces a movement "from climate denial to obstructing climate action" ([openDemocracy](https://www.opendemocracy.net/en/countering-radical-right/the-far-right-has-moved-from-climate-denial-to-obstructing-climate-action/); ["Beyond Obstruction: Rethinking the Far Right and Climate Governance," Global Policy, 30 Jan 2026](https://www.globalpolicyjournal.com/blog/30/01/2026/beyond-obstruction-rethinking-far-right-and-climate-governance)). FrP fits the obstruction model precisely: formal acceptance of climate science combined with systematic opposition to nearly every national policy instrument.

## 9. FrP and the European radical right

### 9.1 The "political only child"

FrP has historically distanced itself from the European far right and turned down alliance offers from other European far-right parties. It belongs to **no European party family or European Parliament group** (Norway is outside the EU) — and, strikingly, it is also **not a member of any party group in the Nordic Council**: the far-right "Nordisk frihet" group (Finns Party, Danish People's Party, Sweden Democrats) has said FrP "has been welcomed to join"; FrP responded that it "does not need a party group" ([norden.org](https://www.norden.org/sv/news/sd-inslappt-i-nordisk-frihet); [Wikipedia: Nordic Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_Freedom)). No evidence was found of FrP affiliation with ECR or Patriots for Europe; Patriots for Europe's 2024 founding membership does not include FrP ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriots_for_Europe); [Euronews, 8 July 2024](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2024/07/08/far-right-patriots-group-springs-to-third-force-in-european-parliament)).

### 9.2 Sister parties refused: SD, DF, AfD

- **Sweden Democrats.** The canonical statement is Listhaug's, September 2022: *"Frp har ingen søsterpartier i Sverige eller noen andre steder"* ("FrP has no sister parties in Sweden or anywhere else") — while simultaneously crediting SD with "the best political solutions" on crime and immigration in Sweden and arguing SD should be included in a bourgeois Swedish government "just as FrP entered government in 2013." She stressed the ideological difference: "FrP is a libertarian people's party… while SD calls itself a socially conservative party" ([Dagsavisen, 9 Sep 2022](https://www.dagsavisen.no/nyheter/2022/09/09/listhaug-om-sd-frp-har-ingen-sosterpartier-i-sverige-eller-noen-andre-steder/)). Beneath the official distance, VG has reported that SD "gets informal help from individuals in FrP" ([VG](https://www.vg.no/nyheter/i/a2Edra/sverigedemokratene-faar-uformell-hjelp-fra-personer-i-frp)), and in 2018 some FrP politicians (notably Tybring-Gjedde) openly wanted SD cooperation ([Nettavisen, 11 July 2018](https://www.nettavisen.no/nyheter/frp-politikere-onsker-samarbeid-med-sverigedemokraterna/s/12-95-3423513121)).
- **Danish People's Party.** Despite shared 1970s Progress-Party roots, FrP's then-international secretary said in 2007 that calling DF a sister party "has no basis in reality… DF belongs to a group of parties we in FrP have absolutely no contact with" ([Danish Wikipedia, citing contemporary reporting](https://da.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremskrittspartiet)). Relations warmed somewhat under Jensen, but no significant 2021–2026 FrP–DF or FrP–Denmark Democrats institutional contacts were documented in this research. Policy-wise, however, **Denmark functions as FrP's chief model**: the loyalty oath, the deportation reform, third-country processing, and crime statistics by origin are all explicitly Danish-inspired (§6, §7).
- **AfD, RN, FPÖ, Fidesz, Vox.** No instance of cooperation was found; FrP's distancing operates mostly by omission and the repeated "no sister parties" line. Norwegian commentary explicitly distinguishes FrP from "Geert Wilders' Freedom Party or Marine Le Pen's National Rally" ([Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/norsk-debatt/hun-har-tatt-for-mye-mollers-tran/o/5-95-3016243)). An outlier: a local FrP lead candidate said in November 2024, "We must hold together and create a cultural and political alliance. In it are Donald Trump, Viktor Orbán and Sylvi Listhaug leading the way" — not endorsed by the leadership ([Firda](https://www.firda.no/jublar-frp-enno-for-trump/o/5-15-1854083)). On Meloni: a French nationalist outlet dubbed Listhaug "la Giorgia Meloni norvégienne" (March 2025), and Listhaug's 2025 push against the European Court of Human Rights' migration jurisprudence parallels the Meloni–Frederiksen open letter ([breizh-info](https://www.breizh-info.com/2025/03/24/245029/sylvi-listhaug-la-giorgia-meloni-norvegienne/); [kjønnsforskning.no, "Amazons of the far Right", May 2025](https://kjonnsforskning.no/en/2025/05/amazons-far-right)).

The comparative pattern: FrP positions itself as *sui generis* — a "libertarian people's party" — refusing the radical-right family label while borrowing the family's policies (above all Denmark's) and benefiting from its discursive space. This is consistent with Ivarsflaten's reputational-shield logic and with the party's history of moderation-through-office.

## 10. FrP and Trump's MAGA: the "Norwegian MAGA" discourse

### 10.1 Leadership distance

The FrP leadership's record on Trump is predominantly one of criticism and pragmatic distance:

- After January 6, 2021, Listhaug (then deputy leader) said: *"Trump er ikke skikket til å være president etter det vi har sett nå"* ("Trump is not fit to be president after what we have seen now"), adding that "Trump has responsibility for inciting these masses over a long time" ([Aftenposten/NTB, 7 Jan 2021](https://www.aftenposten.no/norge/politikk/i/7KVoGv/listhaug-trump-er-ikke-skikket-til-aa-vaere-president)).
- In 2016 she told VG she leaned Republican "but not Donald Trump," citing his NATO and free-trade positions.
- In 2025 she criticized Trump's tariffs as harming Norway, while pragmatically urging PM Støre to "call Trump" and seek an exemption ([VG](https://www.vg.no/nyheter/i/PpxrwR/frp-leder-sylvi-listhaug-om-trump-skader-norge); [Dagsavisen, 27 Feb 2025](https://www.dagsavisen.no/nyheter/innenriks/2025/02/27/listhaug-ber-store-ringe-trump/)).
- Most decisively, after the Trump–Zelensky Oval Office clash she told the Storting on **6 March 2025** that the meeting was "nothing short of disheartening," that "things were said that were deeply unreasonable, which we and our European allies have distanced ourselves from," and that "President Zelensky has led his people in a manner that deserves both respect and support from all who believe in democracy and international law" ([Storting minutes, 6 Mar 2025](https://www.stortinget.no/no/Saker-og-publikasjoner/Publikasjoner/Referater/Stortinget/2024-2025/refs-202425-03-06?m=1)).

### 10.2 Base-level and youth-wing enthusiasm

Against this stands substantial pro-Trump sentiment in and around the party:

- FrP MPs **Christian Tybring-Gjedde and Per-Willy Amundsen nominated Trump for the Nobel Peace Prize in 2018** (Korea diplomacy); Tybring-Gjedde nominated him again in **2020** (Israel–UAE accords) ([PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/far-right-norwegian-lawmaker-nominates-trump-for-nobel-peace-prize); [CBS, Sep 2020](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-nominated-2021-nobel-peace-prize-norwegian-parlaiment-christian-tybring-gjedde/)).
- Listhaug congratulated Trump on his 2024 victory "on behalf of Fremskrittspartiet" ([Facebook, Nov 2024](https://www.facebook.com/listhaugfrp/posts/1131109668370446/)).
- Former deputy leader **Ketil Solvik-Olsen attended Trump's January 2025 inauguration** ([Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/donald-trump/politikk/fremskrittspartiet/tidligere-frp-topp-ketil-solvik-olsen-invitert-til-trumps-innsettelse/s/5-95-2240475)).
- FpU leader **Simen Velle said he would have voted for Trump** (2024); Amundsen backed him; a TV 2 poll showed nearly a third of FrP voters preferred Trump over Harris ([Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/nyheter/fpu-leder-simen-velle-far-stotte-av-per-willy-amundsen-for-trump-uttalelser/s/5-95-1924429); [Dagsavisen, 20 July 2024](https://www.dagsavisen.no/nyheter/innenriks/2024/07/20/amundsen-stotter-velles-trump-uttalelser-en-annen-frp-er-stotter-vaksinemotstander/)).
- Amundsen has also proposed asylum for the British far-right figure Tommy Robinson ([The Nordic Page](https://www.tnp.no/norway/politics/norway-right-wing-former-justice-minister-wants-asylum-in-norway-for-uk-far-right-figurehead-tommy-robinson/)).

No FrP/FpU CPAC attendance in 2024–2026 could be confirmed.

### 10.3 The "norsk MAGA" / "MAGA-fication" debate

Around the September 2025 election, international media described a "MAGA-fication" of Norwegian politics; The Guardian's characterization of the campaign as "Maga-frisert" (MAGA-styled) was relayed and contested in Norway ([Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/nyheter/valget-i-norge-stemples-som-maga-frisert-i-the-guardian/s/5-95-2606237)). Norwegian political scientists largely pushed back. Election researcher **Lise Bjånesøy** (University of Bergen): "Although the FrP did very well in this election… I still don't think I would use the word MAGA-fication… the success of the Progress Party in this election can be explained by domestic Norwegian grievances rather than any Americanization of populist rhetoric" ([ECPS](https://www.populismstudies.org/dr-bjanesoy-frp-turns-economic-frustration-in-norway-into-populist-momentum/)); another Norwegian election researcher called "this MAGA thing… completely off-target" (Nettavisen, above). Earlier domestic uses exist — Vårt Land's editorial "FrPs trumpisme" (17 Dec 2020) ([Vårt Land](https://www.vl.no/meninger/leder/2020/12/17/frps-trumpisme/)) — and some commentators argued the *Centre Party*, not FrP, "most resembles MAGA" ([Altinget](https://www.altinget.no/artikkel/make-norge-great-again)). By May 2026, Norwegian commentary observed that "language giving associations to Donald Trump gives significantly more disadvantages than advantages" — the MAGA association is now treated as an electoral liability that FrP itself avoids ([Nettavisen, May 2026](https://www.nettavisen.no/norsk-debatt/hun-har-tatt-for-mye-mollers-tran/o/5-95-3016243)).

### 10.4 The decisive divergence: Ukraine, NATO, Russia

Unlike much of the MAGA right and parties such as AfD or FPÖ, FrP is firmly Atlanticist and pro-Ukraine: it has backed every Ukraine support package in the Storting and demands that the **Nansen Programme** (NOK 205bn framework to 2030) be *increased*, with Listhaug calling for cuts to non-core state spending to fund more military aid ([frp.no](https://www.frp.no/nyheter/frp-stotter-ukraina); [Altinget](https://www.altinget.no/artikkel/nansen-programmet-maa-oekes-drastisk)). Isolated pro-Putin voices exist at the local level but are marginal ([Nettavisen](https://www.nettavisen.no/ukraina-krigen/vladimir-putin/putin/lokal-frp-topp-forsvarer-putin-krigen-matte-gjores-na/s/5-95-971279)). This foreign-policy profile is the single clearest dimension separating FrP from the MAGA-aligned European right.

## 11. Synthesis: analytic themes for sociological research

Seven themes emerge from the material with direct relevance to a research paper on FrP's crime, migration, and climate policies:

**1. Radicalization of program, softening of register.** The period's defining paradox: the 2025–2029 program contains FrP's most radical immigration platform ever (zero border asylum, third-country residence, quota-refugee stop, deportation regardless of ties) while Listhaug's public register softened ("most immigrants contribute"), the term *snikislamisering* receded, and the party broke through among first-time voters, women (relatively), and even immigrant-background voters. Ivarsflaten's reputational-shield theory and the de-demonization literature (cf. Le Pen's *dédiabolisation*) offer the obvious analytic frames; the Norwegian case adds the twist that the shield is libertarian rather than republican.

**2. Crime as the integrative master frame.** "Svenske tilstander" performs multiple functions simultaneously: it nationalizes a foreign dystopia as imminent domestic threat; it fuses crime with migration without requiring explicitly ethnic claims (the "Swedish" frame carries the racialized content implicitly); and it converts single events (the Bislett grenade, 23 September 2025) into validation of decades of warnings ("Frp fikk rett" — "FrP was right" — is the party's own retrospective slogan). The frame is contested on empirical grounds by criminologists and fact-checkers, and was even inverted by Støre ("Listhaug is helping to create Swedish conditions") — evidence that the frame, not the statistics, structures the debate.

**3. Statistical politics.** FrP's commissioning of SSB tables on charges by country background, its demand for an annual *innvandringsregnskap*, and its "hidden information" complaints constitute a distinctive politics of official statistics: the party positions state statistics agencies as both arbiter and adversary. The December 2024 SSB release — genuinely showing increased overrepresentation in 2020–23 — illustrates how administrative data become political events.

**4. Welfare chauvinism as fiscal nativism.** FrP articulates exclusion through sustainability accounting rather than ethnonationalism — the "costs per immigrant" frame. Since the concept of welfare chauvinism was coined on this very party (Andersen & Bjørklund 1990), the 2021–2026 material offers a longitudinal case of the concept's radicalization: from "welfare for our own first" to "zero asylum immigration" justified by welfare-state survival.

**5. Climate obstruction, not climate denial.** FrP's official line accepts anthropogenic climate change while opposing nearly every national instrument (CO2 tax, ETS2, climate targets, electrification, wind subsidies) and promoting expansion of the resource base (new licensing rounds, LoVeSe). Open denial is tolerated at the margins (Hagen) but is not the party line. The party's 2025–2026 parliamentary wins (repeal of the 2030 neutrality goal, offshore-wind pause) show obstruction operating effectively from opposition. The "cool dudes" finding (Krange et al. 2019) and Sælen's work suggest the climate dimension is constitutive of FrP's electoral coalition, not incidental to it — directly relevant to any analysis treating crime, migration and climate as a single attitudinal syndrome.

**6. Energy-price populism as transition politics.** FrP's most electorally effective climate-adjacent politics concerned electricity prices: "Stopp strømranet," price caps, export controls, anti-cable and anti-electrification positions, and nuclear advocacy. This is distributional transition politics — who pays for decarbonization — rather than climate-science contestation, and it scrambles left–right lines (Rødt and SV share the anti-cable position).

**7. Liminality in the transnational right.** FrP is simultaneously inside and outside the European radical right: no sister parties, no European or Nordic group membership, explicit distancing from SD/DF/AfD/RN — combined with systematic policy borrowing from Denmark, person-level assistance to SD, base-level Trump enthusiasm, and a leadership that polices the MAGA association as a liability. For comparative purposes, FrP is best treated as a *boundary case that strategically performs its own boundary status*: its distinctiveness claims are themselves data. The firm pro-Ukraine, pro-NATO line is the hardest evidence that the distancing is substantive, not merely cosmetic.

## 12. Flagged uncertainties and verification notes

Claims in this report rest on the cited sources; the following items carry reduced confidence and should be independently verified before citation in academic work:

1. **Paywalled/blocked quotations.** Quotes sourced to Klassekampen (8 Jan 2025), some NRK/Aftenposten/VG pieces, and frp.no PDFs were reproduced from search extracts rather than full-text reads. The party program PDFs ([2025–2029](https://www.frp.no/files/Program/2025/FrP-Partiprogram-2025-2029.pdf)) should be read directly for exact program language.
2. **Seat counts.** FrP's 2025 result: 23.9% and 47 seats (final count); election-night reports of 48 seats persist in secondary sources. Labour's gain reported as +1.8/+1.9 pp depending on count stage.
3. **Mudde's verbatim wording** on FrP in *Populist Radical Right Parties in Europe* (2007) was not directly verified against the book itself; the "neoliberal populist / borderline case" characterization was confirmed across multiple academic secondary sources in a dedicated verification pass.
4. **The original Guardian "Maga-frisert" article** was documented only via Nettavisen's report of it; theguardian.com blocked retrieval. The Guardian's Nordic correspondent at the time was Miranda Bryant (election coverage ~8 September 2025), but specific attribution of the MAGA piece is unverified.
5. A claimed Listhaug book titled "Respekt for folk flest" could **not** be confirmed; only *Der andre tier* (2018) is verified.
6. **No FrP/FpU CPAC attendance** (2024–2026) was found — an absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.
7. The June 2026 Utsira Nord offshore-wind pause vote tallies were reported inconsistently (87–82 vs. 51–50), likely referring to different votes.
8. The April 2026 defeat of the *utvisningsreform* rests on a partisan outlet (Document.no); cross-check against stortinget.no voting records.
9. Whether the 14-year criminal-age demand and 50-year maximum were already in the 2021–2025 program (vs. first adopted later) could not be established; they are firmly documented from 2023 and 2025 respectively.
10. The exact day of the February 2025 visitation-zone defeat of the government varies across reports (committee stage ~11–13 February, plenary vote ~20 February 2025); the substance and party constellation (H, FrP, Sp + KrF) are confirmed.
